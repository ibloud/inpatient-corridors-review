# Next Phase — Release Architecture

## Purpose

This page documents the next design phase as an **independent release-architecture study**.

The supplied 17-video sequence is used as a structural reference: a sequence that moves from immediate identity, through variation and expansion, into long-form reflection and documentation.

The public site does **not** reproduce protected recordings, lyrics, artwork, footage, or artist branding. The implementation below preserves the structural lesson rather than presenting third-party material as project assets.

## What this changes in the prototype

The lesson is not “make more assets.” It is to make the **sequence itself part of the experience**.

### Phase A — Hit

The first few pieces should establish identity quickly.

The prototype equivalent is the yellow-door opening: atmosphere first, then social tension, then the player's first meaningful decision.

### Phase B — Vary

Do not ask the player to perform the same cognitive task repeatedly.

Rotate the mode:

**Observe → Converse → Investigate → Reflect → Decide**

This is where the Witness, Caretaker, Archive, and Rumor routes belong.

### Phase C — Reframe

The middle of the experience should change the question.

The restriction can turn out to have a legitimate reason. That does not erase the human problem created by the process.

The question becomes:

> How should decisions be made when they affect people who were not included in making them?

This prevents the story from requiring a villain.

### Phase D — Build

The response should move beyond “who was right?”

Players begin assembling a process:

- shared questions
- support
- information
- participation
- challenge
- infrastructure

The important result is not winning the argument. It is leaving the environment more capable of handling the next disruption.

### Phase E — Remember

The final assets in a release sequence should not simply repeat the climax.

They should create room for:

- retrospective understanding
- documentation
- unresolved questions
- evidence of what changed
- a record that can inform the next iteration

That maps directly to the prototype's retrospective/debrief layer.

## Proposed site treatment

The live project should present this as a **release architecture**, not a conventional campaign page.

The visual treatment should be:

1. A single strong opening statement.
2. A horizontal/vertical sequence of 17 numbered beats.
3. A clear distinction between short-form pieces and long-form anchors.
4. A second layer showing how the same cadence maps onto the interactive prototype.
5. A final “What remains?” section rather than a promotional victory lap.

## Boundary

This is an independent design study. It does not claim authorship, endorsement, participation, approval, or permission from any third party whose work informed the structural reference.

No protected third-party media is required for the prototype to function.

The objective is to demonstrate the **next part of the concept through the work itself**: a richer cadence, more modes of participation, a stronger middle turn, and a retrospective layer that makes the process visible.
