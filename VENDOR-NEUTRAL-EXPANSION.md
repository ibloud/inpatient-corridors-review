# Vendor-Neutral Expansion / Breadcrumb Principle

## Purpose

The Yellow Door / Halloween / Rabbit Hole connection is an **optional creative expansion** of the Inpatient Corridors work. It is not a pitch, procurement request, vendor replacement proposal, or statement about who should build or maintain any artist-facing site.

The intent is to leave a discoverable trail for a future reader, collaborator, rights holder, or creative team:

**Inpatient Corridors → Halloween event → Yellow Door → Rabbit Hole → optional artifacts**

The trail should be understandable without requiring anyone to accept a particular implementation, vendor, platform, or commercial relationship.

## Vendor neutrality

The project must not:

- imply that an existing designer, developer, agency, or retained vendor is inadequate;
- imply that a rights holder should replace an existing vendor;
- use the prototype as leverage for a commercial engagement;
- imply that a visit, review, reply, or silence constitutes approval;
- frame the expansion as a demand for access, endorsement, adoption, or migration.

The project may demonstrate an independent concept and leave breadcrumbs to it. Any future production decision remains with the relevant rights holder and their chosen collaborators.

## Existing relationships are not inferred

This repository does not attempt to determine whether an artist, label, management team, agency, studio, or developer currently has a retainer, contract, preferred vendor, or other working relationship.

If such a relationship exists, the prototype is designed to coexist with it rather than challenge it.

## Creative intent

The Yellow Door is the narrative convergence mechanism. The Halloween/event layer provides a reason to encounter it; the Rabbit Hole provides a connected interactive experience; original project artifacts can be encountered after the transition.

The connection should feel like **discovery**, not solicitation.

## Rights and attribution

The expansion does not establish artist participation, approval, endorsement, authorship, sponsorship, or vendor preference.

Third-party names, music, recordings, artwork, likenesses, marks, or commercial products require their own rights review before public or commercial use.

## Human decision point

If a future rights holder or creative team discovers the work, the appropriate response is to let them decide:

- whether to ignore it;
- whether to review it;
- whether to request changes;
- whether to adopt any idea;
- whether to build it with their existing team;
- whether to build it with another team;
- or whether to do nothing.

No outcome is presumed.
