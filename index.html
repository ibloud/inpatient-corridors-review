This independent Loptr Lab prototype explores institutional pressure, autonomy, community care, and system redesign through a concrete question:

When the institution is gone, what must a community build so people are supported rather than hidden, punished, or abandoned?

The project continues Loptr Lab's original Break the Grid mechanics as a facilitated digital experience. Players move through a system that accumulates pressure, makes suppression costly, and rewards collective redesign over domination. The project would pair the experience with moderated conversations and a public resource layer created with qualified mental-health and lived-experience partners.

If Chris drives the direction

The proposed experience becomes an independent-artist pressure lab: speak plainly, test inherited rules, own the consequences of rupture, build support without surrendering agency, and preserve a record that improves the next attempt. This is Loptr Lab's interpretation for Chris to approve, correct, or reject.

Proposed pilot

  • A 15–20 minute browser-based scenario built from original Loptr Lab mechanics and art.
  • Three facilitated community sessions with small, consented cohorts.
  • A resource page developed with qualified community-care partners.
  • Anonymous, minimal evaluation: completion, voluntary reflection, accessibility feedback, and intended next action.
  • A public process archive showing what changed because participants spoke.

This is an arts-and-engagement prototype — not therapy, diagnosis, medical advice, crisis care, or clinical research.

Recognition strategy

The project is designed first to be useful and accountable. If it produces real public benefit, its strongest current recognition path is the Anthem Awards — Health, whose judging emphasizes concept, execution, impact and engagement, inclusivity, and capacity building.

No award, nomination, eligibility, or individual credit is guaranteed. No submission should be made without written approval from the credited parties and evidence that the public work occurred.
