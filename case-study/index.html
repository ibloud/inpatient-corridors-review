TRANSPARENT WORKING RECORD

What was proposed.
What was built.
What remains.

A calm, inspectable case-study surface for reviewing the work behind 50 Ways to Leave Another. This is not a victory lap and not an attack on another vendor. It separates public evidence, our own demonstrations, and questions that remain unanswered.

Open the project
01 / CLIENT EVALUATION

Judge the record, not the pitch.

A reviewer should be able to distinguish what was actually delivered, what we independently demonstrated, and what cannot be established without private source material or production access.

We do not have the original Ren brief or production repository. Therefore this case study does not claim to know what the site was “supposed” to be.

02 / EVIDENCE

Three evidence layers.

PUBLIC RECORD

Current public site behavior and published agency/client case-study material. These are external observations, not our reconstruction of a private brief.

OUR BUILD

Yellow Door and Rabbit Hole provide inspectable evidence of browser narrative runtimes, structured content, reproducible builds, accessibility-oriented interaction and portable artifacts.

UNKNOWN

Private architecture, production credentials, private analytics, original briefs and undocumented requirements remain unverified. We say so.

REVIEW

The client can inspect the record without being asked to accept a claim merely because it appears in a presentation.

03 / PUBLIC BENCHMARK

What the published record actually says.

Creative Deploy publicly describes its Sick Boi work as a headless Shopify + Sanity system and documents a content-management workflow intended to let the team adapt drops with minimal developer involvement. Its published case study also reports high-traffic results for that project.

That is a documented benchmark for Sick Boi. It is not evidence of Ren's private brief, nor proof that renmakesmusic.com uses exactly the same implementation.

The live Ren site independently shows editorial, music, shop, events and interactive-experience surfaces. Those observations establish what is publicly visible; they do not establish the private architecture behind them.

Sources: Creative Deploy — Sick Boi case study · Creative Deploy — work · Ren Makes Music

04 / THE GRAVEYARD

What failed is part of the record.

The graveyard records discarded approaches, broken builds, false starts and unresolved questions. Its purpose is not blame. Its purpose is institutional memory.

  • Yellow Door: Ink compilation and browser-runtime work documented as a reproducible build.
  • Rabbit Hole: three-room narrative prototype, structured content fixtures, validation, Pages deployment and itch packaging.
  • Ren reconstruction: stopped at the case-study/reference boundary. No claim of production ownership or access.
  • Unknowns: anything not directly observed remains an open question.
05 / MONEY

Expenses are expenses.

The proposed compensation principle for this body of work is deliberately transparent: recover actual, documented expenses first. The remaining value generated by the work is intended to support the Made-Sick organization and its mission rather than being hidden inside an opaque project fee.

RECOUPActual documented expenses
RECORDReceipts, services, hosting and production costs
REMAINDERMade-Sick organization

This describes a proposed operating principle, not a statement of tax, nonprofit, employment or charitable-law status.

06 / SELF-EMPLOYMENT

Universal Basic Income as a working idea.

“UBI: self-employment” is used here as a project philosophy: create useful work, disclose what it costs, retain agency over the work, and make the economic result legible.

It is not presented as a claim about current law or a guaranteed income program.

07 / THE BLACK HOLE

Where creative value becomes difficult to see.

“The Black Hole” is a project term for the opaque gap between creative work, money spent around that work, and the people who actually made it. The case study asks where value goes, what can be documented, and which parts of the system can be made inspectable.

This is a framing device, not a claim that “Black Hole” is a formal entertainment-industry term.

08 / REVIEW

Safe enough to review in public.

A useful review lets Ren, a collaborator, an agency, a funder or an audience distinguish:

  1. what was actually delivered;
  2. what is demonstrated but not production-ready;
  3. what remains a proposal or open question.

The objective is transparency without humiliation: a client should be able to say, “I understand what I paid for, what exists, what remains, and what the evidence supports.”
