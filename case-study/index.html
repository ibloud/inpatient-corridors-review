TRANSPARENT WORKING RECORD

What was proposed.
What was built.
What remains.

A calm, inspectable case-study surface for reviewing the work behind 50 Ways to Leave Another. This is not a victory lap and not an attack on another vendor. It is the record.

Open the project
01 / CLIENT EVALUATION

The question is simple.

Can a reviewer understand the proposed outcome, inspect the evidence, see what did not make the cut, and decide whether the work delivered enough value?

The evaluation should be possible without trusting a pitch deck, a private conversation, or a claim that cannot be reproduced.

02 / EVIDENCE

Show the work, not the story about the work.

RESULT

Working prototypes, source repositories, builds, tests and documented decisions.

PROCESS

Failures stay visible when they explain a decision or prevent the same failure from returning.

LIMITS

Unverified claims are marked unverified. Production access we do not possess is not presented as access we possess.

REVIEW

The client gets a record that can be shown to collaborators, funders, partners or an audience without requiring a private explanation.

03 / THE GRAVEYARD

What failed is part of the deliverable.

The graveyard records discarded approaches, broken builds, false starts and unresolved questions. Its purpose is not blame. Its purpose is institutional memory.

  • Yellow Door: browser-runtime and Ink compilation work documented as a reproducible build.
  • Rabbit Hole: three-room narrative prototype, content fixtures, validation, Pages deployment and itch packaging.
  • Ren: stopped as a production reconstruction and retained as a case-study/reference exercise rather than pretending to own the production system.
  • Unknowns: anything not directly observed remains labelled as an open question.
04 / MONEY

Expenses are expenses.

The proposed compensation principle for this body of work is deliberately transparent: recover actual, documented expenses first. The remaining value generated by the work is intended to support the Made-Sick organization and its mission rather than being hidden inside an opaque project fee.

RECOUPActual documented expenses
RECORDReceipts, services, hosting and production costs
REMAINDERMade-Sick organization

This page describes the proposed operating principle, not a statement of tax, nonprofit, employment or charitable-law status.

05 / SELF-EMPLOYMENT

Universal Basic Income as a working idea.

Here, “UBI: self-employment” is a project philosophy: people should be able to create useful work, disclose what it costs, retain agency over the work, and make the economic result legible.

It is presented here as a principle for discussion, not as a claim about current law or a guaranteed income program.

06 / THE BLACK HOLE

The economy people in entertainment describe when the money disappears.

“The Black Hole” is used here as a project term for the opaque gap between creative work, money spent around that work, and the person who actually made it. The case study asks where value goes, what can be documented, and which parts of the system can be made inspectable.

This is a framing device, not a claim that “Black Hole” is a formal industry term.

07 / REVIEW

Review it in public without making the client unsafe.

A useful review should let Ren, a collaborator, an agency, a funder or an audience distinguish three things:

  1. what was actually delivered;
  2. what is demonstrated but not production-ready;
  3. what remains a proposal or open question.

The goal is a record a client can stand behind even when the answer is “this part did not work.”
