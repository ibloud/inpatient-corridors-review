# Inpatient Corridors: Break the Grid

## A consent-first collaboration proposal for Chris Webby / Inpatient

**Status:** Independent review draft from Loptr Lab. Not affiliated with, approved by, or endorsed by Chris Webby, Ren, Inpatient, EightyHD Music, or their representatives.

This repository proposes a small, playable public-interest project connecting the themes listeners encounter in *Asylum*—neurodivergence, medication, institutional control, addiction, friendship, and survival—to a concrete question:

> When the institution is gone, what must a community build so people are supported rather than hidden, punished, or abandoned?

The proposal adapts Loptr Lab's existing **Inpatient Corridors** concept into a facilitated digital experience. Players move through a system that accumulates pressure, makes suppression costly, and rewards collective redesign over domination. The project would pair the experience with moderated conversations and a public resource layer created with qualified mental-health and lived-experience partners.

## What Chris is being asked to consider

Chris is not being asked to endorse a finished product or serve as a mental-health-policy expert. He is invited to decide whether any part of this should proceed and, if so, in what role:

1. **Decline** — no name, music, likeness, or implied relationship will be used.
2. **Review only** — give private creative or factual feedback without public affiliation.
3. **Creative adviser** — help shape the voice, experience, and boundaries with written credit terms.
4. **Co-creator / principal participant** — enter a negotiated agreement covering rights, approval, compensation, safeguarding, credit, and award attribution.

Ren's participation is separately optional and cannot be inferred from Chris's response.

## Proposed pilot

- A 15–20 minute browser-based scenario built from original Loptr Lab mechanics and art.
- Three facilitated community sessions with small, consented cohorts.
- A resource page developed with qualified community-care partners.
- Anonymous, minimal evaluation: completion, voluntary reflection, accessibility feedback, and intended next action.
- A public process archive showing what changed because participants spoke.

This is an arts-and-engagement prototype, **not therapy, diagnosis, medical advice, crisis care, or clinical research**.

## Recognition strategy

The project is designed first to be useful and accountable. If it produces real public benefit, its strongest current recognition path is the **Anthem Awards — Health**, whose judging emphasizes concept, execution, impact and engagement, inclusivity, and capacity building. A secondary future path may be a Webby category appropriate to the finished digital work.
