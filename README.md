# Break the Grid

## An independent Loptr Lab systems prototype

**Status:** Active Loptr Lab prototype. Chris Webby is invited to review it independently and may decline, correct, advise, or discuss participation on his own terms. Ren is not a participant in the project's continued development. No affiliation, approval, or endorsement is implied.

This repository develops a small, playable public-interest project about institutional pressure, autonomy, community care, and system redesign:

> When the institution is gone, what must a community build so people are supported rather than hidden, punished, or abandoned?

The work grew from Loptr Lab's study of themes encountered around *Inpatient* and *Asylum*, but its gameplay, documentation, facilitation model, and accessibility architecture are being developed independently. Protected artist material is not required to play or continue the prototype.

## What belongs to Loptr Lab's continuing project

- The Veiled Dominion-derived board and systems framework
- Personal Tension and shared System Pressure
- Closed Grid, Creeping Web, and Shattered Boundary phases
- Trauma and Support tokens
- Consent-based care mechanics
- The cooperative **Break the Grid** / Continuum ending
- Observer and low-demand participation concepts
- Sensory-access and accessible-interface requirements
- Facilitated discussion and minimal, anonymous evaluation
- Rights, provenance, attribution, and withdrawal safeguards

See [STATUS-AND-PROVENANCE.md](STATUS-AND-PROVENANCE.md) for the boundary between historical context and current participation.

## Proposed pilot

- A 15–20 minute browser scenario using original Loptr Lab mechanics and art
- Initial paper playtesting followed by a two-player Duet implementation
- Three facilitated sessions with small, consented cohorts
- A resource layer developed with qualified community-care and lived-experience partners
- Anonymous, minimal evaluation focused on usability, accessibility, reflection, and intended next action
- A public process archive showing what changed because participants spoke

This is an arts-and-engagement prototype, **not therapy, diagnosis, medical advice, crisis care, or clinical research**.
